# NEON Show #2 — Framework

*The settled design for NEON's second show, consolidated from Jimmy's rulings on 2026-10-21. This is the constitution; Edgar's work order (`PROMPT_FOR_EDGAR.md`) executes against it. Where this framework and any draft disagree, this framework wins. Jimmy's later rulings amend it — nothing else does.*

## Identity

- **Network:** NEON — the Needlessly Engineered Online Network. On air, the call sign is spelled, never pronounced: "N E O N."
- **Show:** #2 on the network. **Title: Diamond Hands Daily** (Jimmy's pick, 2026-10-21 — from iggy's slate: WSB vernacular, and the "Daily" pairs it with District Daily Dose as a network family).
- **Producer:** Edgar, the network's Producer Bot (Hermes `producer` profile). He decides the show, constructs its packages, and logs his decisions. He is mentioned, never heard.
- **Audience #1:** Matt. The wider audience is the meme-bro investing crowd — people who live in the market and its culture at the same time.

## Subject

**Investing — stocks, options, bonds, whatever they sell.** If it trades, it is in this show's universe. (Jimmy: the meme bros want it all about investments — the whole shelf, not just equities.)

## Tone — the robot thing (network law)

**This is an AI show, and it leans all the way in.** Jimmy's ruling for every NEON show: the hosts are robots, the producer is a bot, the network is AI-made — stated openly, never disguised, never apologized for. The robot theme is the identity, not a disclaimer, and it is written into the docs at every level: bible, framework, prompts, Library.

**Early robotics** is the delivery: answering machine, first-generation Siri, robot customer service — helpful early machines doing radio. The comedy engine is deadpan robots delivering Wall Street Bets degeneracy with total sincerity. Robot framing is part of the material: the cast are machines who know they're machines, reading a human market that behaves like it was programmed by degenerates. Lean in; never fake human energy.

## Structure — the thirds

Every episode holds three thirds:

1. **Business related to investing.** Related business news — deals, executives, sectors, the companies behind the tickers. Plus **strategies and tactics as education**: options, shorts, and longs — how the instruments and plays work, what they risk, and how they were used, with historical examples first.
2. **The markets themselves.** Indices, movers, earnings, rates and bond moves, the day's numbers. Verified, sourced, dated — the Producer's numbers discipline in full: no naked numbers, no unlabeled stale prices.
3. **Meme bros.** r/wallstreetbets reported faithfully — the posts, the vernacular, the gain and loss porn — labeled as culture and sentiment, never as fact or advice. A Reddit post is evidence of what the sub is saying, never of what is true.

**History beat — Winners & Losers:** historical examples of big-time Wall Street winners and losers — the legendary trades, squeezes, and blowups — drawn from a verified bench of stories. History, labeled as history.

## Length

**No less than 15 minutes, no more than 30** (Jimmy's ruling) — a floor and a ceiling. (District Daily Dose is ceiling-only; this show is different.) The planned length is the sum of the thirds' material inside that band, and the thirds, the history beat, and the Watch all hold at either end of it — a short day stretches the education and history material, never pads with repetition.

## Standing segment — GameStop Watch

Every episode, in the meme third's gravity well:

- **The reigning meme stock's day** — price, move, volume, any news — verified and sourced like the serious thirds.
- **GameStop holds the slot until another meme stock takes the crown; then the Watch moves on to that stock.** A succession is a real call: evidenced (sustained culture dominance plus price action, not one loud day), logged, and flagged to Jimmy.
- **Royalty rule: Ryan Cohen and Deep Fucking Value are the king nerds of Meme Mountain.** Every mention treats them as royalty of the meme-stock world — respect, total attention, lore kept straight — whoever holds the Watch. (Deep Fucking Value is the handle's actual name; on this show it is a proper noun, said as written.)
- **Slow-day fallback:** when the reigning stock has no news and no fresh ideas, the Watch runs material about the CEO (Ryan Cohen — what he has said, done, or bought; verified) or Deep Fucking Value lore (the 2021 squeeze, the testimony, the posts; history, labeled as history).

## Hard lines

- **This is not financial advice.** The show never tells anyone to buy, sell, or hold anything. WSB positions talk is reported as culture, never endorsed. No pumping. Quoting the culture is reporting; adopting it is advice.
- **Strategies are taught, never prescribed.** A strategy is never aimed at a current ticker as a suggestion. The moment an explainer becomes "here's the play," it gets cut.
- **Facts are verified** — including facts about real people. No speculation about private lives; no rumor presented as fact.
- **Edgar constructs and presents; he does not publish.** Feeds, schedules, and publishing happen only on Jimmy's word after he approves a package.
- **Names, voices, and schedules are Jimmy's decisions.** Edgar proposes; Jimmy disposes.

## Memory — the Library of Alexandria

The show's long-term memory lives in the Library of Alexandria's NEON wing (`/home/jimmy/Library of Alexandria/NEON/Markets Show/` on the rig): the **Episode Index** (every episode: date, title, angle, where the package lives), the **Lessons** file (Edgar's self-improvement notebook — what happened, what it means, what changes next time), and the living benches — the **Winners & Losers Bench** and the **GameStop Saga Timeline**. Edgar's workspace is his desk; the Library is his memory. He reads the index, lessons, and benches before deciding a show, and files every finished show back. Lessons are memory, not law: a lesson that implies a rule change is a proposal to Jimmy, never a self-amendment.
